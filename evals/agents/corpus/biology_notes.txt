BIOLOGY 101 - CLASS NOTES (Semester 1)

1. Photosynthesis
Photosynthesis happens in the chloroplasts of plant cells. It has two stages.
The light reactions take place in the thylakoid membranes. Light energy splits water, which releases
oxygen, and the energy is stored in two carriers: ATP and NADPH.
The Calvin cycle takes place in the stroma. An enzyme called RuBisCO attaches carbon dioxide to a
five-carbon sugar. Using the ATP and NADPH from the light reactions, the cycle turns the carbon
dioxide into a three-carbon sugar (G3P), which the plant uses to build glucose.

2. Cellular respiration
Respiration releases the energy stored in glucose. It has three stages.
Glycolysis happens in the cytoplasm. It splits one glucose into two pyruvate and gives a net gain of
2 ATP. The Krebs cycle happens in the mitochondrial matrix and produces NADH and FADH2. The electron
transport chain sits in the inner mitochondrial membrane and makes most of the ATP, about 30 to 32 ATP
per glucose in total. Oxygen is the final electron acceptor.

3. DNA replication
Replication is semi-conservative: each new DNA molecule keeps one old strand and gets one new strand.
Helicase unwinds the double helix. Primase lays down a short RNA primer. DNA polymerase adds new
nucleotides, always in the 5' to 3' direction. The leading strand is made continuously. The lagging
strand is made in short pieces called Okazaki fragments, which DNA ligase joins together.

4. Cell division
Mitosis makes two genetically identical diploid cells and is used for growth and repair.
Our class mnemonic for the phases of mitosis is PMAT: Prophase, Metaphase, Anaphase, Telophase.
Meiosis makes four genetically different haploid cells (gametes) through two divisions.
Crossing over in prophase I of meiosis shuffles genes between homologous chromosomes.

5. Movement across membranes
Diffusion moves particles from high to low concentration with no energy cost. Osmosis is the diffusion
of water across a semi-permeable membrane. Active transport moves particles against their gradient and
needs ATP. Cells in a hypotonic solution gain water and may swell.

6. Enzymes
Enzymes are biological catalysts. They lower the activation energy of a reaction. Each enzyme has an
active site that fits its substrate (the induced-fit model). Temperature and pH change an enzyme's
shape; beyond its optimum the enzyme denatures and stops working.
