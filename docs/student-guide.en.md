# Student guide

MindNest AI helps you study from your own notes: ask questions and get answers with sources, practise with quizzes, get help understanding assignments, and plan your revision. This page explains each part in a few minutes.

## 1. Getting started

Create an account with your email and a password of at least 10 characters, or press Continue with Google. If you used email, open the link we send you to confirm your address.

**Try it**

1. Choose your language (English, हिन्दी or ગુજરાતી) from the language menu at the top. You can change it any time.
2. Add your AI key in Settings (see the next part). Without it the AI features cannot answer.
3. Create a subject, upload your notes, then ask your first question in Chat Assistant.

<a id="key"></a>

## 2. Your AI key

MindNest AI does not include an AI service. It uses an account you own with an AI provider (Anthropic, Google Gemini, OpenAI or Groq) through a code called an API key. This keeps the cost under your control. Some providers have a free or low-cost tier; check the provider's own page for current prices.

Your key is encrypted before it is stored and is never shown again after you save it. Anyone who has your key can spend your money, so never share it, and delete it from Settings if you think it leaked (you can also delete it at the provider).

**Try it**

1. Open Settings, then AI providers.
2. Pick a provider. The link next to the key box takes you to the page where that provider creates keys.
3. Paste the key, choose a model (or leave the default), and press Save. Use Test to check that it works.

> **Tip:** If answers stop with an error about your key, the usual reasons are an expired key, no credit left at the provider, or a model name your account cannot use.

## 3. Subjects and materials

A subject is one course, such as Physics or Economics. Put its notes inside it: PDF files, Word documents, PowerPoint slides, text files, and even photos or scans of pages (the text is read automatically, including Hindi and Gujarati).

After you upload a file it shows Processing for a short while, and Ready when it can be searched. Large or scanned files take longer. If a file shows an error, use Retry; if it keeps failing, try a smaller file or a clearer scan.

Inside a subject, tabs show Overview, Modules, Materials, Notes, Previous papers, Quizzes, Assignments and Analytics. Use Modules to group files by unit and study one unit at a time. Analytics shows your quiz scores, weak topics and a suggestion for what to do next.

**Try it**

1. Open My Subjects and press the button to create a subject.
2. Open it and upload your files.
3. Wait for Ready on each file before asking questions about it.
4. Give each file a module name so you can study unit by unit.

> **Tip:** Deleting a subject deletes its materials. The app tells you what will be removed before it does anything.

<a id="library"></a>

## 4. Materials Library

The Materials Library is a shared shelf for your college and for everyone. Add your college name in Settings, under Account, so classmates from the same college can find what you share.

You publish a copy of your own file, so changing or deleting your original does not affect it. You choose who can see it: only you, your college, or everyone (public items are checked by an administrator first). You can edit or withdraw what you published, add someone else's item to your own subject, and report anything that looks wrong.

**Try it**

1. Open Materials Library and choose College, Others, Everyone or Mine.
2. Press Publish on one of your own files and choose who can see it.
3. To use someone else's item, press Add to my subject.

> **Tip:** Only publish what you have the right to share: your own notes, or material that is free to pass on.

## 5. Asking questions in Chat Assistant

Choose a subject (or all your subjects) and ask in your own words, in English, Hindi or Gujarati. The answer is built from your uploaded materials, and numbered sources show where each part came from. Click a number to see the passage.

If your notes do not contain the answer, MindNest says so instead of guessing. You can then add the missing material, or ask for general help. Always treat the answer as a study aid: AI can still be wrong, so check important facts in your textbook.

You choose what Chat may search: all your subjects, one subject, or only certain files. You can also attach a PDF, Word file or picture to a single conversation (up to 5 files, 15 MB each); it is used for that conversation only.

Under the last answer you can press Regenerate to have it written again (the earlier versions are kept), and give it a thumbs up or down so you remember what helped. Suggested follow-up questions appear under an answer, and Chat can draw simple diagrams such as trees, linked lists and graphs.

**Try it**

1. Pick the subject at the top of the chat.
2. Type your question. Ask follow-ups in the same conversation.
3. Open the numbered sources to check the answer against your own notes.
4. Use the search control above the question box to pick the files Chat may use.

> **Tip:** Ask for a diagram, a summary, or a step-by-step explanation. Specific questions get better answers than very broad ones.

<a id="quizzes"></a>

## 6. Quiz and Practice

MindNest can write a quiz from your materials: multiple-choice, true or false, and short answers. You choose the subject, how many questions, and how hard. Short answers are marked by the AI with a short explanation, so read the explanation as well as the score.

Your results show which topics are weak. Cards you got wrong come back for revision at sensible intervals, and the Study Planner uses your weak topics when it builds your plan.

**Try it**

1. Open Quiz & Practice and press the button to create a quiz.
2. Wait while it is written, then start an attempt.
3. Submit, read the review, and come back to the revision cards when they are due.

<a id="assignments"></a>

## 7. Assignment Helper

Paste the question or instructions from your teacher, add the due date, and choose what help you want: Break it down (what is being asked and a step-by-step plan), Suggest an outline (a structure for you to fill in, pointing to the right parts of your notes), or Feedback on my draft.

The helper guides you and checks your work against your notes. It does not write the assignment for you, and that is on purpose: you learn by writing it yourself, and your teacher will want your own work.

Each assignment has tabs: Overview, Files, Instructions, Rubric, AI assistance, Draft feedback and History. Drag files onto the page to add them; the Files tab shows the progress of each upload. The Overview tab tells you the next step to take.

**Try it**

1. Open Assignment Helper and add the assignment.
2. Choose Break it down first, then the outline.
3. Write your draft, paste it in, and ask for feedback. Improve it and ask again.

> **Tip:** Your draft is sent only to the AI service you chose, with personal details hidden first.

<a id="planner"></a>

## 8. Study Planner

Add your exam dates. The planner then makes a list of study tasks from your exams, assignment deadlines, revision cards that are due, and the topics your quizzes show are hardest, with the weakest material first. You can tick tasks off, add your own, and make the plan again at any time; finished tasks are kept.

Tell it how much time you have each day in the planner preferences, so the plan is realistic.

The planner has five views: Today, Week, Month, Timeline and Tasks. Cancel a task you will not do; you can restore it later. If a day has more planned than the time you set, a warning appears with a lighter day to move a task to.

When you make the plan again, your own tasks, tasks you finished or cancelled, and tasks you moved or resized are kept. Only the automatic, unfinished tasks from today onward are replaced.

**Try it**

1. Open Study Planner and add your exams.
2. Check your daily time in the preferences.
3. Press Make my plan and work through today's tasks.

<a id="calendar"></a>

## 9. Calendar and Google Calendar

The Calendar page shows your exams, assignment deadlines and planned study tasks in one place.

If you want them on your phone too, you can connect Google Calendar. MindNest then creates a separate calendar called MindNest AI and can only see and change that one, never your other calendars. Nothing is sent until you press Review changes and tick the items you want. Events are all-day and do not block your time.

You can disconnect any time, and choose to delete the MindNest AI calendar from Google as well.

Choose Month, Week, Day or Agenda to see your days the way you like. Press Add event to put your own event on the calendar, with or without a time. You can tick “Send to Google Calendar” on an event to have it sent as well.

Every item shows whether it is synced, waiting or failed. A failed item says why and has a Retry button. Sending the same item twice never creates a duplicate on Google Calendar.

**Try it**

1. Open Calendar and press Connect Google Calendar.
2. Accept the permission on Google's page.
3. Press Review changes, tick what you want, and send.

## 10. Study Groups

A study group is a private shared shelf for your class or friends. Only people who have the group's join code can see what is inside, and each group is separate from the others.

To share, pick one of your own materials and add it to the group. Members can download it or add a copy to their own subjects. Only share what you have the right to share: your own notes, or material that is free to pass on. Do not upload copyrighted books or exam papers you were not given permission to share.

If you see something wrong, use Report. When several people report an item it is hidden until an administrator looks at it. If you leave a group, the files you shared are removed from it.

Roles: the owner can make someone an admin. Owners and admins can remove members, pin or lock discussions, and delete anything in the group. Everyone else can add and read, and change their own work.

A group page has tabs. Files holds shared materials. Notes are shared text everyone can read. Discussions are for questions and answers. Study sessions have a time and place: say Going, Maybe or Can't go, and press Add to my calendar to put the session on your own calendar.

You can also be invited by email. If someone invites the email address you signed up with, an Invitations for you box appears on the Study Groups page, and you choose Join or Decline. An invitation stays open for 14 days.

**Try it**

1. Open Study Groups and either create a group or enter a join code you were given.
2. The person who created a group can see its code and can make a new code if the old one spread too far.
3. Share a material, or download one from a classmate.
4. To invite a classmate, open the group, go to Members and enter their email address.

> **Tip:** Files from other people are not checked for you. Be careful with anything you do not trust.

<a id="eval"></a>

## 11. Evaluation Lab

The Evaluation Lab measures how well MindNest finds the right passage in your own notes. It is optional and meant for anyone who wants to check the quality of search, for example before an exam or for a project.

A question set is a file of questions with the words in your notes that answer each one. A run asks every question with a search method and records how often the right passage was found. Tick two or more runs to compare them. The What the scores mean tab explains every number. Searching costs nothing; only the option to write answers uses your AI key.

**Try it**

1. Open Evaluation Lab and press Import question set.
2. Press New run, choose the search methods, and start.
3. Open a finished run to see each question and the passages that were found.

## 12. Settings and language

Settings has six tabs: Account (your name, language, college and time zone), Security (change your password, see where you are signed in, download your data), AI providers, Retrieval models, Calendar and Privacy. Your time zone decides what counts as today in the planner, the calendar and revision cards.

You can switch between English, Hindi and Gujarati at any time from the language menu. Menus, messages, dates and times all change together, and your choice is remembered on your other devices. Some of the Hindi and Gujarati wording was written with the help of AI; if something reads oddly, tell the person who runs your MindNest.

**Try it**

1. Open Settings and check your college and time zone under Account.
2. Open Security to change your password or sign out other devices. Your password is checked first, and every other device is signed out when you change it.
3. Use the language menu at the top to change the language.

<a id="privacy"></a>

## 13. Your privacy and staying safe

Your notes and questions are sent to the AI service you chose, under that service's own rules. Before anything leaves MindNest, Aadhaar, PAN, card numbers and secret keys are always hidden, and email addresses, phone numbers and UPI ids are hidden too unless you turn that off in Settings. The AI sees a label such as [PHONE] instead.

Instructions hidden inside a document (for example text saying ignore the rules) are treated as plain text and are not followed, and answers are checked so that tracking links, outside pictures and scripts are removed. These protections catch the common cases, not every case, so please also use common sense.

No other student can see your subjects, chats or files unless you share a material in a study group. Administrators can see counts and account details to run the service, never your content.

**Try it**

1. Never paste passwords, bank details or photos of ID documents into any AI tool.
2. Use a password you do not use anywhere else, and sign out on shared computers.
3. If something looks wrong, tell your teacher or the person who runs your MindNest.

<a id="problems"></a>

## 14. When something goes wrong

A file stays on Processing for a long time: wait a few minutes, then press Retry. If it still fails, the page may be unreadable; try a clearer scan or convert it to PDF.

No answer, or an error about your key: open Settings, press Test on your provider, and check that your provider account has credit.

The answer seems to ignore your notes: make sure the right subject is chosen and the file shows Ready, and try asking with words that appear in your notes.

You cannot sign in: use Forgot your password on the sign-in page. If you used Continue with Google before, use the same button again.

Anything else: take a screenshot and send it to the person who runs your MindNest.
